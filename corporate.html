The BRAT Methodology

Built on performance practice. Adapted for the workplace.

BRAT translates selected tools from established acting, voice, movement and improvisation practices into a practical system for workplace communication. No character work. No forced performance. Participants learn to manage tension, use their voice deliberately and respond to what is actually happening in the room.

01

Intentional Presence & the First 30 Seconds

Draws from Stanislavski, Uta Hagen & Michael Chekhov

The idea
Shift attention from “How do I look?” to a concrete purpose: clarify, uncover, connect or set a boundary.
The practice
Prepare the first 30 seconds and use physical grounding to establish a clear tone.
The result
Employees enter a room or call knowing what they need to accomplish, with less self-monitoring.
02

State & Tension Control

Draws from Alexander Technique & Laban

The idea
Build physical range instead of memorizing one “correct” confident pose.
The practice
Notice automatic tension, pause and release unnecessary effort before speaking.
The result
Professionals can settle more quickly and communicate with a calmer, more grounded presence under pressure.
03

Vocal Command & Pacing

Draws from Linklater voice work & Viewpoints

The idea
Use breath, pause, pace and resonance to make the main thought easier to follow.
The practice
Work with vocal pace and group dynamics to choose when and how to enter the conversation.
The result
Employees can make a point without rushing, straining their voice or losing the room.
04

Real-Time Adaptation & Status Dynamics

Draws from Meisner, Spolin & Johnstone

The idea
Move beyond a private mental script and engage with what is actually happening.
The practice
Listen to the end, notice changes in energy or participation and adapt without losing the objective.
The result
Teams are better prepared for difficult stakeholders, unexpected questions and complex room dynamics.

What People Practise

Four practical skills people can use immediately at work.

The goal is not to remove every nerve or manufacture a confident persona. It is to give people practical choices when pressure changes how they speak, move, think and respond.

Body · Breath · Attention

Prepare & Settle

Use a short routine before a call, meeting or group interaction to arrive with more access to breath, voice and attention.

Voice · Pace · Emphasis

Make the Point Land

Use breath, pace, pause and emphasis so the main thought is easier for other people to follow.

Attention · Partner

Notice & Listen

Move attention from self-monitoring or preparing the next answer to the task, the room and the other person.

Response · Recovery

Adapt & Continue

Respond when conditions change and continue after an unexpected question, blank, interruption or mistake.
