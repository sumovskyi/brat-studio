The BRAT Methodology

Built on performance practice. Adapted for the workplace.

BRAT translates established acting, voice and movement practices into practical workplace tools. No character work or forced performance—just ways to manage tension, make the point heard and adapt in real time.

Informed by Stanislavski, Laban, Linklater, Meisner and other established performance practices.

The words may be prepared. BRAT prepares the person who has to deliver them.

01

Intentional Presence

Shift attention from “How do I look?” to a clear purpose: clarify, connect or set a boundary.

Work result: Employees enter meetings with a clear objective and less self-monitoring.

02

State & Tension Control

Release unnecessary physical tension before speaking instead of forcing a confident pose.

Work result: Calmer, more grounded communication when pressure rises.

03

Vocal Command & Pacing

Use breath, pause and pace to make the main point easier to follow.

Work result: Ideas land without rushing, straining or over-explaining.

04

Real-Time Adaptation

Drop the internal script. Listen, read the room and adapt as conditions change.

Work result: Better recovery from interruptions, difficult questions and shifting room dynamics.

The Skills & Business Impact

What employees practise—and the practical value the organization receives.

Four repeatable skills for moments when pressure changes how people speak, move, think and respond. Each skill is tied to a clear workplace use.

Body · Breath · Attention

Prepare & Settle

The practice: Use a short routine before a high-stakes call or meeting to arrive with full access to your breath, voice, and attention.

Company value: Capable employees gain a practical preparation tool instead of being told to “just be confident.”

Voice · Pace · Emphasis

Make the Point Land

The practice: Use deliberate pacing, pauses, and vocal emphasis so your main thought is impossible for others to ignore.

Company value: Existing expertise is easier to bring into meetings and client conversations, with less time lost to over-explaining.

Attention · Partner

Notice & Listen

The practice: Shift your focus entirely from self-monitoring (preparing your next answer) to the room and the actual task at hand.

Company value: Shared practice and simple cues support more responsive collaboration with less friction in daily teamwork.

Response · Recovery

Adapt & Continue

The practice: Adapt in real time when conditions change and recover smoothly after an unexpected question, interruption, or mistake.

Company value: Employees practise useful flexibility for work moments that cannot be fully planned.
